# Decisions that preserve scientific meaning

## Scope and decomposition

Record the original requirements before seeing results. A question leaf has a purpose and an acceptance condition, not just keywords. Identify whether the user needs a mechanism, a comparison, an outcome, a boundary condition or a feasibility judgment. Definitions that determine eligibility or units precede the related comparisons; background questions need not block independent evidence collection.

When material is missing, preserve the question and mark its limitation. Do not change it to fit easy evidence. On a fixed corpus, a request for a search protocol can require a **prospective** plan; clearly separate proposed searches from searches actually executed.

## Retrieval and stopping

Use exact query, provider, filters, pagination and corpus/version in an action fingerprint. Query normalization must not discard a scientific sign, unit, date filter or phrase boundary. Two different providers are not interchangeable cache entries. Retry and new pagination are distinct from wasteful duplicates.

Inspect metadata for relevance, then the actual passage used by a claim. Link preprint/revised/final versions without automatically counting them as independent studies. Multiple experiments in a source may yield several evidence records. Reprints and news reports of one experiment are not replications.

Prioritize mandatory missing answers, material contradictions and high-impact claim verification. A bounded targeted follow-up may stop after two unproductive reformulations as a starting heuristic; record what changed and what remains unresolved. Stop because of time, inaccessible full text, exhausted allowed materials or no new relevant evidence using distinct reasons. Do not imply exhaustive systematic screening from a convenience search.

## Evidence and calculations

Save the source passage, not only a model summary. Web headings and paragraph/line spans are acceptable when pages are absent; PDF locations must correspond to the actual file. Preserve original and normalized units, numerator/denominator, sample size, uncertainty and time point when they affect comparison. Use the raw table's row/column labels, including footnotes, for derived calculations.

Do not infer significance from point estimates, causation from association, generalization from one benchmark or long-term effects from a short follow-up. If a comparison requires unavailable information, report it instead of performing an unjustified pooled calculation.

## Conflicts

Compare these fields before interpreting different conclusions: object/population, design/method, intervention/dose, comparator, outcome definition, units, measurement/follow-up time and relevant conditions. Classify:

- compatible results under comparable conditions;
- materially incompatible findings under sufficiently comparable conditions;
- heterogeneous settings, not a direct contradiction;
- insufficient information to decide comparability.

Keep supporting and opposing passages even when one study has stronger directness. Explain a preference using the actual method and applicability, not citation count. A keyword negation detector is only a candidate finder. A model relevance score is not risk of bias or certainty of scientific truth.

## Audit and bounded repair

Check both directions: each claim has adequate evidence, and each original requirement has a supported answer or an explicitly unresolved disposition. Include uncited factual sentences in review. Traceability is separate from entailment; quotations can be perfectly located while interpretation is wrong.

Use explicit semantic review verdicts and rationale. Rewrite or qualify only failing material, recheck changed claims and retain unresolved requirements. All reviews, tool retries and targeted searches count toward the same budget. A small sample of reviewed claims must not be reported as a whole-report support rate.

For development comparisons freeze required questions and answerability before inspecting model output. Report supported answer coverage together with claim support. An empty or cautious answer cannot earn perfect completion. A correct uncertainty statement for an unknowable question is useful, but remains separate from supported answers to answerable questions.
